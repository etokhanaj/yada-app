# Human audio and plain lookups lead v2

Yada's best v2 additions are three resources that are free to use commercially, work offline and serve the passage. The first is **human BSB narration, dedicated to the public domain under CC0, with word timings Yada generates itself**. The second is a **"who, where, what" lookup** built from the CC BY-SA Tyndale/Aquifer Open Bible Dictionary, STEPBible's CC BY 4.0 proper-noun data and OpenBible's CC BY 4.0 place identifications. The third is a **plain "missing from older manuscripts" note** drawn from the STEPBible TAGNT edition flags Yada already ships, with the CC BY 4.0 SBLGNT apparatus behind it. Each answers a need found in the earlier reader research: audio is the second most praised value in five-star reviews, 57% of churchgoers find the Bible hard to understand alone, and "missing verses" breeds distrust. Each also fits the passage-first, typographic reader without new visual language. On the platform side, v2 should add a few low-cost iOS features: widgets, the Assistive Access full-screen key, and a Live Activity while audio plays. App Intents wait for Expo SDK 58. Maps, images and timelines look attractive but carry the most trouble. The best map set is about 1 GB of finished artwork. Most free Bible images are non-commercial. The only open timeline dataset follows one Ussher-style chronology with almost no flags on contested dates. These belong in v3 or later, rebuilt as type rather than pictures. Three rules filter the whole field. Share-alike licences bind any data Yada edits, though not its code. Several "open" datasets carry AI-written prose that Yada must flag or drop. And under rule 9, fields carrying ESV or NIV names, together with Apple Intelligence surfaces, need deliberate handling.

## Licences sort the field into four bins before quality does

Nearly every candidate falls into one of four licence bins, and the bin decides how much work shipping takes. **Public domain and CC0** carry no obligations. This bin holds the Berean Standard and Berean Majority Bibles ("Licensing is not required for any use" since 30 April 2023) ([berean.bible](https://berean.bible/licensing.htm)), the BSB audio narrations ([bereanbible.com/audio](https://bereanbible.com/audio/)), the Open English Bible ([OEB README](https://github.com/openenglishbible/Open-English-Bible)), the Aquifer edition of BDB ([BDB metadata](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/BibleAquifer/BDBHebrewLexicon/main/eng/metadata.json)), Abbott-Smith and Dodson ([Abbott-Smith](https://github.com/translatable-exegetical-tools/Abbott-Smith); [Dodson](https://github.com/biblicalhumanities/Dodson-Greek-Lexicon)), and Natural Earth basemaps, which need no credit at all ([Natural Earth](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/about/terms-of-use/)). **CC BY 4.0** needs only a credit line. This bin holds OpenBible's geocoding ([readme](https://github.com/openbibleinfo/Bible-Geocoding-Data/blob/main/readme.md)), all of STEPBible-Data including TIPNR and TFLSJ ([STEPBible-Data](https://github.com/STEPBible/STEPBible-Data)), the SBLGNT and its apparatus ([SBLGNT](https://github.com/LogosBible/SBLGNT)), and CNTR's Statistical Restoration Greek text ([CNTR](https://greekcntr.org/resources/)).

The third bin, **CC BY-SA 4.0**, holds most of the modern, readable material. It covers the Tyndale Open Bible Dictionary ([Tyndale Open Resources](https://tyndaleopenresources.com/)) and its Aquifer adaptation, the UBS semantic dictionaries, the Biblica map sets, Theographic, ACAI, all four unfoldingWord helps, the ULT and UST, and the FBV. Shipping BY-SA files inside a paid app is fine. The share-alike duty attaches to any data Yada adapts, so a reformatted dictionary must itself be published under BY-SA; Yada's app code is not affected. unfoldingWord adds a trademark clause on top: any modified copy "must remove the unfoldingWord® trademark" ([en_ult LICENSE](https://git.door43.org/unfoldingWord/en_ult/raw/branch/master/LICENSE.md)). Yada should therefore label adapted texts neutrally. The fourth bin is **excluded outright**. Bible Brain's audio and verse timings require that end users get access "completely free of charge" and forbid offline use outside its download endpoint ([FCBH licence](https://www.faithcomesbyhearing.com/bible-brain/license)). FreeBibleimages sets other than Sweet Publishing mostly forbid commercial use or derivatives ([FreeBibleimages reuse](https://www.freebibleimages.org/help/reuse/)). Meta's MMS forced-alignment weights are reported as CC BY-NC 4.0 ([chordai](https://huggingface.co/chordai/mms-fa-aligner-coreml)). The NET Bible sends commercial publication through HarperCollins ([netbible.com](https://netbible.com/copyright/)), so if Yada ever added it, it would be a display-only licensed translation under rule 9.

Two problems cut across the bins. The first is **AI-written prose inside open data**. TIPNR's Brief, Short and Article descriptions say they were "Adapted from the output by Claude 3 Opus AI in April 2024" (TIPNR file header in [STEPBible-Data](https://github.com/STEPBible/STEPBible-Data)). Under Yada's rule that generated content ships only as `flagged`, those fields are out, and only TIPNR's structured names, references, relations and Strong's numbers can be used. The Biblica maps' entity tags are machine-matched, with confidence around 0.3–0.6 and `review_level` "None" ([BiblicaOpenBibleMaps](https://github.com/BibleAquifer/BiblicaOpenBibleMaps)). The second is **unclean licence chains**. Theographic's people data partly descends from a TIPNR release that was CC BY-NC and from genealogy websites of unknown licence ([Theographic bibliography](https://github.com/robertrouse/theographic-bible-metadata/blob/master/docs/data-source-bibliography.md)). Taking people data straight from the current CC BY TIPNR is the safer route. TIPNR's own header also asks users not to redistribute the data and to note any changes. That sits awkwardly with CC BY 4.0's ban on extra restrictions, and a short email to STEPBible, which the header invites, would settle it.

## Human BSB audio with generated word timing is the biggest single gain

Audio is the most visible gap between Yada and what readers reward. In the earlier review analysis, "audio" was the second most praised value after daily habit, with 340 mentions in five-star reviews. Charging for audio drove much of the backlash at ESV and Dwell (see the [ESV App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id361797273) reviews). The BSB has a ready answer. Bob Souer, Barry Hays and Jordan Gilbert have each recorded full-Bible narrations, and "These audio versions of God's Word have been dedicated to the public domain (CC0 1.0)" ([bereanbible.com/audio](https://bereanbible.com/audio/)). Under CC0, Yada can host the files on its own CDN, re-encode them and bundle them. The files are large: Souer's 66 book MP3s add up to about **5.7 GB** ([openbible.com/audio/souer_books](https://openbible.com/audio/souer_books/)). A newer "Frederick Surrey" set comes as one MP3 per chapter, from 238 KB for Psalm 117 to 13 MB for Psalm 119, or 3.9 GB in total ([openbible.com/audio/bsb_frederick_surrey](https://openbible.com/audio/bsb_frederick_surrey/)). Its narrator and its CC0 coverage are unconfirmed, because the folder has no licence file and the official page names only the other three readers. The offline model is therefore one chapter downloaded on demand and kept, not the whole Bible at install. Speech re-encoded at a low bitrate should shrink the total a great deal, though that estimate has not been measured.

None of the open recordings ships with timings, so Yada has to make its own. Because the text is known exactly, this is forced alignment rather than transcription, which is the easier problem. A permissive stack exists: WhisperX (BSD-2-Clause) ([depscope](https://depscope.dev/pkg/pypi/whisperx)) or the Montreal Forced Aligner (MIT) ([PyPI Stats](https://pypistats.org/packages/montreal-forced-aligner)), with an English CTC model reported as Apache-2.0 ([chordai](https://huggingface.co/chordai/mms-fa-aligner-coreml)). aeneas is AGPL and aligns only at sentence level ([PyPI](https://pypi.python.org/pypi/aeneas/1.6.0.0)), and the MMS weights are non-commercial. Alignment should run in the build pipeline on a Mac and ship per-word start and end times as bundle data keyed to Yada's word ids; verse timings come out of the word timings for free. The research notes estimate one to three days for the pipeline and its QA tooling. That estimate is untested, so the plan should include spot checks on Psalm 119, the genealogies and Hebrew names. Under rule 5, alignment output is generated data: words with low confidence go to John for review rather than shipping silently. One open question has to be settled before any of this. Each narration must match the BSB text revision Yada ships, and the notes could not establish which revision each recording follows.

Apple's speech stack stays as the fallback for WEB, KJV, LSV and any chapter without timings. iOS 26's SpeechAnalyzer gives on-device word time ranges, with a vendor-reported boundary error of about 113 ms ([desert-ant-labs/align](https://huggingface.co/desert-ant-labs/align)). It transcribes rather than aligns, though, so timings computed ahead of time are simpler and more accurate. Personal Voice could let a reader hear scripture in their own voice. It needs explicit permission, cannot render to buffers and is unreliable in the background ([Apple forums 736148](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/736148)), so it is an opt-in extra, not the audio plan. WEB audio is public domain but patchy: the ebible.org MP3 page covers only the NT and Psalms ([ebible.org](https://ebible.org/webaudio/mp3.htm)), and its full-Bible zip credits no narrator ([ebible.org/eng-web/audio](https://ebible.org/eng-web/audio/)). Volunteer LibriVox KJV recordings are public domain in the US ([LibriVox](https://librivox.org/dramatized-bible-stories-of-the-old-testament-by-king-james-version-kjv/)), but quality varies. Both are v3 candidates at best.

Reading plans belong with audio because both serve the "make my reading go somewhere" job, and both can be built with no dates. YouVersion had to add "Catch Me Up" to stop users "constantly being told you are behind" ([The Sweet Setup](https://thesweetsetup.com/use-catch-feature-youversions-reading-plans/)). The habit research points the same way. In the "what-the-hell effect", believing you have broken a rule is enough to derail self-control ([EFPSA](https://more.efpsa.org/rpblog/?p=1172)), and a single missed day reportedly "did not materially affect" habit formation ([UCL Discovery](https://discovery-pp.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1477627)). Yada's plan format should therefore be an ordered list of passages with no days: "Next: Genesis 3 · Matthew 3", completion ticked per passage, and progress that only grows. The 1842 M'Cheyne plan is public domain ([wd.bible](https://wd.bible/en/plan/detail/40)). Crossway's version is copyrighted ([esv.org](https://esv.org/plans/one-year-tract)), so Yada should build from the original, add a canonical order and a "Gospels" route, and write its own "after John" route. No licensed open chronological plan was found, so a chronological order would have to be Yada's own.

## Words, people and places become one-line lookups beside the passage

The second v2 cluster answers the 57% who struggle to read alone. It gives a plain answer to "who is this, where is that, what is a threshing floor", and nothing more. The strongest source is the **Aquifer Open Bible Dictionary**: 6,130 English articles, adapted from the Tyndale Open Bible Dictionary, CC BY-SA 4.0, as JSON and Markdown, last pushed 5 October 2026 ([metadata](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/BibleAquifer/AquiferOpenBibleDictionary/main/eng/metadata.json)). It is modern reference prose aimed at non-experts, and its attribution must name both Tyndale House Publishers and Mission Mutual ([README](https://github.com/BibleAquifer/AquiferOpenBibleDictionary)). The public-domain 19th-century dictionaries, Easton's, Smith's and the 1915 ISBE, are free of obligations, but their archaeology is out of date and their prose sounds dated. They fail Yada's plain-words test.

For people, **TIPNR** is the most precise disambiguation source. It has about 4,255 records, each with a unique verse-anchored tag, every Hebrew and Greek form, disambiguated Strong's numbers, family links and an exhaustive reference list, all under CC BY 4.0 and with commits as recent as 10 October 2026 ([STEPBible-Data](https://github.com/STEPBible/STEPBible-Data)). It lets Yada print "Zechariah, son of Jehoiada, a priest (2 Chr 24)" instead of leaving the reader guessing among 26 men of that name. That count comes from Theographic, which gives a sense of the scale: 459 names are shared by more than one person, and only 286 of its 3,067 people are marked "publish" rather than "wip" ([Theographic field docs](https://github.com/robertrouse/theographic-bible-metadata/blob/master/docs/json-fields-documentation.md)). Theographic's family graph is useful, but its BY-SA licence and unfinished state make it a cross-check, not the source. TIPNR's own "(?)" markers, where a decision rests on an ambiguity, should show in the UI as "identification uncertain". Its AI-written descriptions stay out.

For places, **OpenBible's geocoding** is unusually honest data for an evidence-led app. It covers 1,341 ancient places, drawn from more than 70 modern sources and citing over 400, with confidence votes and a time-weighted score in which "An overall total of 500 or higher represents high confidence" ([readme](https://github.com/openbibleinfo/Bible-Geocoding-Data/blob/main/readme.md)). Measured locally, 876 places score high, 459 score mid-range and 7 score low. That supports a typographic place line with no map at all: "Bethel, about 18 km north of Jerusalem; location confident", or "location uncertain; scholars differ". This applies rule 4 directly, since the line states only what the data contains. The repo was last updated in November 2021, so it is a stable snapshot.

For words, the **UBS Dictionary of the Greek New Testament** (5,507 entries) and the **UBS Dictionary of Biblical Hebrew** (7,932 entries) are the rare find. Both are semantic-domain lexicons descended from Louw-Nida, and both are CC BY-SA 4.0 ([Greek metadata](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/BibleAquifer/UBSGreekNTDictionary/main/eng/metadata.json); [Hebrew metadata](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/BibleAquifer/UBSHebrewDictionary/main/eng/metadata.json)). They arrange senses by context, which is exactly what the earlier research found lay word studies lack: readers otherwise pour every gloss into one word or fall for the root fallacy ([Logos, Mark Ward](https://www.logos.com/grow/better-resources-than-strongs-concordance/)). Paired with MACULA's sense data, they let Yada say what this word means in this verse. The other lexicons fit expert screens better than v2. STEPBible's TFLSJ gives full LSJ entries under CC BY with no share-alike, Perseus LSJ is BY-SA plus a request to share changes ([PerseusDL](https://github.com/PerseusDL/lexica)), and BDB is CC0. Yada's TBESG already embeds corrected Abbott-Smith, so raw Abbott-Smith adds little.

The translation helps fit later. unfoldingWord's Translation Words (953 key-term articles such as "grace" and "covenant") are the most reader-friendly of the set, and its Translation Questions are simple comprehension prompts ([UWTranslationWords metadata](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/BibleAquifer/UWTranslationWords/main/eng/metadata.json)). Translation Notes, at 87,573 entries, is mostly figure-of-speech labels and alternative renderings written for translators. The Dictionary of Bible Themes (2,530 entries, licensed to Biblica for CC BY-SA release) ([metadata](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/BibleAquifer/DictionaryBibleThemes/main/eng/metadata.json)) is a topical index, a useful "more on this theme" link but not a v2 need. All of these are grounding-eligible for Yada's pipeline, because CC licences carry no AI clause. Whether a guide grounded in BY-SA sources must itself be BY-SA is a policy question John has to decide. It already applies to the Tyndale Open Study Notes in place, and each new BY-SA grounding source widens it.

## Textual variants turn a distrust trigger into a reviewed note

"Why is this verse missing?" is a recurring one-star complaint; Matthew 17:21 is the usual example. Yada can answer it with data it already holds. STEPBible's TAGNT marks every word with the editions that contain it, covering NA27/28, TR, SBLGNT, Tregelles, Byzantine, Westcott-Hort and THGNT, along with positional and meaning variants ([STEPBible-Data](https://github.com/STEPBible/STEPBible-Data)). That is enough for a quiet line under Mark 16:9–20 or John 7:53–8:11: "Not in the earliest manuscripts; most modern editions bracket it". The line needs no new licence. The **SBLGNT apparatus**, CC BY 4.0 since the December 2022 relicence ([SBLGNT](https://github.com/LogosBible/SBLGNT)), adds a compact record of which edition reads what at each variant unit, for example "χριστοῦ WH ] + υἱοῦ θεοῦ Treg NA28; υἱοῦ τοῦ θεοῦ RP" at Mark 1:1 ([apparatus](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LogosBible/SBLGNT/master/data/sblgntapp/text/Mark.txt)). Yada would translate the sigla into plain words and state only what the apparatus states, under rule 4. CNTR's raw manuscript transcriptions are now plain CC BY-SA 4.0 ([CNTR](https://greekcntr.org/resources/)), but they are expert material for a later "earliest manuscripts" view. THGNT has no open licence. The variant note is a v2 item because it costs little and feeds Compare and the guide's "where readers differ" directly. It still needs a reviewed short explanation per major variant, so the apparatus alone is not enough.

More translations matter less than they first seem. Yada already has a literal text (LSV), two older texts (KJV, WEB) and a readable modern one (BSB). The gap is a **plain-language text** for readers who find even the BSB hard. The **UST** fills it: meaning-based, CC BY-SA 4.0, revised in September 2026, and adapted from Translation for Translators ([en_ust LICENSE](https://git.door43.org/unfoldingWord/en_ust/raw/branch/master/LICENSE.md)). eBible no longer lists it, so Yada would take it from Door43 or the Aquifer mirror ([eBible](https://ebible.org/find/details.php?id=engult)). The trademark clause means a reformatted copy must drop the unfoldingWord name. The FBV (CC BY-SA, modern idiom) ([eBible engfbv](https://ebible.org/find/details.php?id=engfbv)) is a second option. The OEB's CC0 status is tempting, but its Old Testament is incomplete. The ULT duplicates the LSV's literal role, and T4T reads as translator scaffolding. A plain text belongs in v3, after the core lookups, because the guide already supplies plain words. Its main job would be as a Compare column showing a sentence said simply. One housekeeping item belongs in v2: eBible lists the LSV as "copyright © 2020 Covenant Press" under CC BY-SA 4.0 ([eBible englsv](https://ebible.org/find/details.php?id=englsv)), and Yada's existing attribution should match that wording.

## Maps, images and timelines need rebuilding as type before they fit

The visual resources are where open licensing and Yada's design rules clash most. The **Biblica Open Bible Maps** are the largest commercial-safe set: 540 English maps under CC BY-SA 4.0, each tied to passages ([BiblicaOpenBibleMaps](https://github.com/BibleAquifer/BiblicaOpenBibleMaps)). But at about **1.08 GB of finished PNGs** they defeat offline-first, and their artistic style, with no visual review recorded, may not suit a typography-led reader. FIA Maps adds 76 more under BY-SA, with conflicting copyright holders listed in the README and the inventory ([FIAMaps](https://github.com/BibleAquifer/FIAMaps)). The better path for Yada is to draw its own minimal maps. It can combine public-domain Natural Earth coastlines and rivers with OpenBible coordinates and confidence scores, rendered in the app's own type and colour tokens and fully offline. OSM detail would bring ODbL share-alike and attribution duties on any derived database ([OSM](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright)), a cost not worth paying at Bible-map scale. The research gives no effort estimate for drawing these maps. Building an accessible map, with VoiceOver order, Dynamic Type labels and contrast in both themes, is plausibly the largest single design job on this list, which is why it sits in v3.

Images split cleanly. The useful ones explain unfamiliar things, a cistern or a threshing floor, rather than decorate. **UBS Images** (1,054 English items) is labelled both CC0 and CC BY-SA 4.0, with no per-item field, so the whole set should be treated as BY-SA ([UBSImages](https://github.com/BibleAquifer/UBSImages)). OpenBible's site thumbnails carry a licence and credit per image: Yada must ship only the CC0, PD, CC BY and CC BY-SA ones and never the 300 Google Street View links ([readme](https://github.com/openbibleinfo/Bible-Geocoding-Data/blob/main/readme.md)). Sweet Publishing's cartoon illustrations are commercial-safe under CC BY-SA 3.0 ([FreeBibleimages](https://www.freebibleimages.org/illustrations/jacob-wedding/)), but they clash with the typography-led look. Public-domain fine art from Commons can be added file by file, and a 2023 UK Court of Appeal ruling clarified that photographing a public-domain artwork creates no new copyright, which helps an England and Wales company ([Commons PD-Art](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Reuse_of_PD-Art_photographs)). Realia images inside the dictionary lookup are the only image use that clearly earns its place, and they belong in v3 alongside the maps.

Timelines are the weakest field. **Theographic's 450 events** are the only substantial open dataset. They follow one chronology that sets creation at 4004 BC, after Floyd Nolen Jones, and only 6 events carry the `rangeFlag` that marks a date as approximate ([field docs](https://github.com/robertrouse/theographic-bible-metadata/blob/master/docs/json-fields-documentation.md); [bibliography](https://github.com/robertrouse/theographic-bible-metadata/blob/master/docs/data-source-bibliography.md)). Shipping those years bare would claim a precision the scholarship does not support, against the spirit of rule 4. No open dataset records alternative chronologies, such as an early versus late Exodus, with flags on disputed dates. The orientation need the reader research found, knowing where a passage sits in the larger story, is better met in v2 with relative order ("after the exile", "during Solomon's reign") written into the reviewed guide. A visual timeline waits until Yada has hand-reviewed anchor dates with ranges.

## iOS integrations add presence without nagging, if rule 9 is handled

Four platform features give the most value for the effort, and none needs an account, notifications or a streak. **Widgets via expo-widgets** work on Yada's current SDK 57, generating the extension and App Group from config ([expo-widgets v57](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/v57.0.0/sdk/widgets.md)). A "Continue reading" widget and a passage-of-the-day widget fed by a precomputed offline timeline also appear in StandBy and, on iOS 26, in CarPlay ([Apple forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/forums/topics/app-and-system-services/app-and-system-services-widgets-and-live-activities)). The library is still labelled alpha, so versions should be pinned. The **Assistive Access full-screen key** is one Info.plist line, `UISupportsFullScreenInAssistiveAccess` ([WWDC25 238](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/238/)), and it matches Yada's accessibility rule exactly. A **local Live Activity during audio playback**, started on play and ended on stop, also appears on the Lock Screen, in the Dynamic Island, in the Apple Watch Smart Stack and on the CarPlay Dashboard ([HIG](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/design/human-interface-guidelines/live-activities.json)). That removes most of the case for a Watch app or a full CarPlay app. The read-along already exists, so this fits v2 even before human audio ships. Yada's native audio module will still need to publish Now Playing information itself. **App Intents and Spotlight** ("Open John 3", "Continue reading") need `expo-app-intents`, which exists only from SDK 58 and in alpha ([v58 docs](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/v58.0.0/sdk/app-intents.md)), so they arrive with the SDK upgrade.

The verse-widget app category routinely bundles streak trackers ([From Bible](https://mwm.ai/apps/app/6759759625)). Yada's widgets must show a passage and "continue", never a day count or a ring. The sharper risk is **rule 9 on Apple's AI surfaces**. Spotlight entity descriptions, Siri responses and on-screen entity association must carry only references and BSB, WEB, KJV or LSV text, never NIV, NKJV or NLT verses. The conservative policy is also to turn off Writing Tools on views that show licensed text. Whether React Native text views expose Writing Tools, and how to opt out, is unverified and needs a check. Any intent that returns guide text must return only `approved` entries. The same caution applies to data: TIPNR's translated-name fields and Theographic's `esvName` should stay out of every prompt, with KJV and BSB names used instead. CarPlay as a full app, SharePlay, iCloud sharing and an Apple Watch app are each high effort, and the first two add social or entitlement complexity for little gain at Yada's stage. A TTS-only feature may not even qualify for the CarPlay Audio entitlement ([Apple CarPlay entitlements](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/carplay/requesting-carplay-entitlements.md)).

## Conclusion

The research turns what looked like a hunt for content into a question of editing. Commercially usable open material now covers almost every category Yada might want, and most of it is maintained, structured and dated 2025–2026. The constraint is no longer what may legally ship. It is what holds up under Yada's own rules once it ships. That is why the visually richest resources rank lowest. A thousand finished maps, a precise-looking timeline and an entity layer with AI-written summaries each assert more than their evidence supports, or more than Yada's typographic style can absorb. The plainest resources, a confidence score, an edition flag or a CC0 recording, rank highest because they carry their own honesty.

Two decisions sit upstream of the whole roadmap and should be made before v2 work starts. First, Yada needs a share-alike policy: whether BY-SA grounding makes generated guide text BY-SA, and where Yada will publish the adapted data files BY-SA requires. Second, Yada needs a written rule 9 policy for the operating system's AI surfaces, not just its own pipeline, because widgets, Spotlight and Siri create new paths for licensed text to reach a model. Settling both early keeps v2 from setting precedents that v3 would have to undo.
