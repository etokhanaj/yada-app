# How lay believers study the Bible: behaviours, blockers, and what makes it click

> **Method note for the report writer (read first).** Reddit was the main suggested source, but none of it could be reached. reddit.com, old.reddit.com and the JSON endpoints returned "blocked by network security" (403). The PullPush and Arctic Shift archives were rate-limited or down. The Redlib mirrors sit behind a bot-detection challenge, which was deliberately not bypassed. Web search also returned no Reddit threads. ChristianForums.com and PuritanBoard returned 403 to the fetcher.
>
> What follows comes from lay forums that could be reached (ChristianityBoard), lay-written blogs and Substacks with reader comments, and survey data from the American Bible Society, Lifeway Research and Barna/Gloo. That survey data is the strongest evidence of what laypeople report. Teacher and ministry articles appear only where they describe lay behaviour or warnings.
>
> **Coverage is therefore thinner on verbatim lay voices than the brief asked for.** No Reddit quote appears anywhere in these notes. A summariser model extracted the quotes from fetched pages. They should be spot-checked against the source before publication.

## 1. How do lay readers say they study?

### Takeaway
The default lay method is a daily reading plan, often Bible-in-a-year in an app, done alone. When people find that too fast, they move to slower formats: fewer chapters, one book at a time, audio, two-year plans, or children's storybook Bibles. Some also add journaling. AI use is now mainstream among practising Christians: Barna/Gloo report that roughly 4 in 10 to nearly half say AI has helped their Bible study or prayer. Their trust in it is ambivalent.

### Cited Findings
- About two-thirds of US Bible users access the Bible digitally. State of the Bible 2025 says Bible study groups are associated with higher "flourishing" — [Scripture Engagement summary of State of the Bible USA 2025](https://scripture-engagement.org/content/state-of-the-bible-usa-2025/)
- Motivations (ABS 2025): 47% use the Bible "because it brings me closer to God"; 20% for wisdom in decisions; 15% for comfort; 3% "I know I'm supposed to"; 2% because a group or school requires it — [Decision Magazine on State of the Bible 2025](https://decisionmagazine.com/more-than-half-of-americans-wish-they-would-read-the-bible-more)
- A young lay writer quit a Bible-in-a-year plan because she could not absorb or take notes at that pace, and it became box-ticking. Her four alternatives: an audio Bible in a year, a Bible in two years, a Bible in three months, and a children's storybook Bible in a year. Commenters (Hallie Daniel, Michael Goddard II, KJ Lynn) agreed that slowing down helped. One commenter (Bojidar Marinov) defended structured plans. Quote: "My thoughts became more focused on checking off that day's reading than meditating." — Tabitha Bell, March 2023 — [The Rebelution](https://therebelution.com/blog/2023/03/why-i-quit-my-bible-in-a-year-plan-and-4-alternatives/)
- Lay forum thread (Dec 2024 – Jan 2025): some posters follow year-long daily plans, others adapt plans to their schedule or build their own, and one gave up formal plans for "Spirit-led" study. Quote: "Start on track. Stay on track. … Fall off track? Get back on track." — Matthias, 30 Dec 2024 — [ChristianityBoard: Bible reading plans](https://www.christianityboard.com/threads/bible-reading-plans.67257/)
- SOAP (Scripture, Observation, Application, Prayer; from pastor Wayne Cordeiro) is widely taught and paired with journaling. However, the evidence found was church and publisher how-to pages, not lay testimony — [Thomas Nelson Bibles](https://thomasnelsonbibles.com/?p=30303); [2 Pillars Church](https://2pillarschurch.com/blog/2020/4/soap-learning-to-read-the-bible-and-journal)
- AI use (Barna/Gloo; surveys Nov 2025, n=1,514 US adults, and Dec 2025, n=442 Protestant pastors; released 20 May 2026):
  - Nearly half of practising Christians say AI has helped with Bible study, prayer or spiritual growth.
  - 34% of practising Christians, and 44% of Millennials, say AI's spiritual guidance is as trustworthy as a pastor's.
  - 48% of practising Christians would trust AI for spiritual growth.
  - Researcher Daniel Copeland: Christians are "extending trust and registering fear in the same breath."
  — [Gloo press release](https://gloo.com/press/releases/ai-is-becoming-a-spiritual-authority-even-among-practicing-christians)
- A second report of the same Barna work gives the rate of AI use for faith questions as 22–25% of practising Christians. That is lower than the "nearly half helped" headline, so the two framings conflict and the writer should hedge. It also reports 41% of pastors using AI for sermon or Bible-study preparation, and 12% of pastors feeling comfortable teaching about AI — [Baptist Standard](https://baptiststandard.com/news/faith-culture/barna-research-suggests-more-christians-are-adopting-ai/)
- Ministry writers describe ChatGPT as "like a concordance you can query": useful for concrete textual questions, weak on contested theology, and liable to invent facts. They advise lay users to check claims against a commentary — [Michael Bird, "Brave New Bible"](https://michaelfbird.substack.com/p/brave-new-bible-ai-and-bible-studies); [Christianity.com](https://www.christianity.com/wiki/current-events/can-christians-trust-ai-for-bible-study.html)
- BibleProject (Tim Mackie and Jon Collins; videos since about 2014–15) makes animated overviews of every book, plus theme videos, word studies, podcasts and classes. It shows up repeatedly as a lay entry point to the big-picture storyline — [Wikipedia: BibleProject](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BibleProject); [YouVersion BibleProject OT explainers collection](https://www.bible.com/videos/collections/1704-old-testament-overview-explainers)

### Inferences
- The behaviour pattern is: start with an app plan, decide the pace is wrong, then slow down or change format. The real job is "make this daily habit feel like it is going somewhere", not "finish the plan".
- AI is already part of how laypeople study. The open question is trust and sourcing, not whether they use it. Practising Christians say they want sources shown (see section 4).

### Gaps
- No Reddit evidence on the mix of methods: inductive study, study Bibles, commentaries, podcasts, chronological plans, reading a book straight through. The ABS public summaries also lack a method breakdown. The full report at sotb.americanbible.org was not retrieved.
- No lay testimony found that SOAP or inductive study improved retention. Only instructional sources were found.

## 2. What makes people stop or fail?

### Takeaway
The top reported barriers are lack of time, not knowing where to start, low motivation, and language that feels hard to relate to. A majority of churchgoers also say the Bible is hard to understand on their own. The guilt cycle around plans and streaks is a well-documented quitting trigger. Leviticus and Numbers are widely called the point where whole-Bible plans die. A separate group stops because of how the Bible has been used against them, not because of comprehension.

### Cited Findings
- State of the Bible 2025 (ABS): 52% of Americans wish they read the Bible more. Top barriers:
  - "never seem to have enough time": 26%
  - "don't know where to start": 17%
  - "don't feel that excited": 15%
  - "language difficult to relate to": 15%
  — [Decision Magazine](https://decisionmagazine.com/more-than-half-of-americans-wish-they-would-read-the-bible-more)
- State of the Bible 2023: Bible use (3+ times a year) fell from 50% in 2020 to 39%. A separate survey (Equipping Godly Women) lists time, energy, motivation, distraction, forgetfulness and insufficient Bible knowledge as obstacles — [K-LOVE, 28 Apr 2023](https://www.klove.com/news/faith/why-arent-americans-reading-the-bible-42592)
- Lifeway Research (with Explore the Bible): 57% of US Protestant churchgoers find it challenging to make sense of the Bible on their own (19% strongly, 38% somewhat). Yet about 81% feel confident helping others with doubts or a confusing passage. Scott McConnell: "most admit they stumble on understanding the specific meaning as they read." Dated 2020, older than the preferred window but the most direct comprehension statistic found — [Baptist Courier, Sept 2020](https://baptistcourier.com/2020/09/churchgoers-confident-in-the-bible-but-sometimes-find-it-challenging/); [Bible Gateway blog, Aug 2020](https://www.biblegateway.com/blog/2020/08/half-of-usa-churchgoers-admit-they-have-confusion-over-the)
- Guilt and streaks: a pastor describes falling behind a plan by mid-January and building up guilt. He tells of someone who stopped reading the Bible entirely after breaking a 100-day streak — [YMI, Jon Coombs, 5 June 2023](https://ymi.today/2023/06/its-time-to-take-the-guilt-out-of-your-bible-reading/)
- Plan pace prevents retention. Reading fast meant no absorption and no notes, and the plan felt like a checklist (Tabitha Bell). One commenter (KJ Lynn) finished a Bible-in-a-year but found deeper understanding hard at that speed — [The Rebelution, March 2023](https://therebelution.com/blog/2023/03/why-i-quit-my-bible-in-a-year-plan-and-4-alternatives/)
- Leviticus is called the "widow maker" of read-through-the-Bible-in-a-year plans: after the stories of Genesis and Exodus, readers meet regulations such as how to tell whether a sore is infectious — [Goodreads author blog](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/5368214-leviticus-the-read-through-the-bible-in-one-year-widow-maker?tab=book). Beginner guides routinely advise against starting at Genesis for this reason — [Noel in UAE Substack](https://noelinuae.substack.com/p/i-want-to-start-reading-the-bible-where-do-i-begin-317772)
- Lay forum threads titled "I'm in a Bible reading slump" and "Tired of reading the Bible..." exist on ChristianForums.com. Search snippets describe an OP who cannot find motivation even after praying. Full text was blocked (403), so no quote is included — [ChristianForums: slump](https://www.christianforums.com/threads/i%E2%80%99m-in-a-bible-reading-slump.8145558/)
- A ministry article lists the drivers: wrong expectations and tools, emotional barriers (grief, anger at God), confusion about where to start, no help with context and continuity, isolation, and Bible reading tied to condemnation. Quote: "The Bible is not a scoreboard. It is a living word." — [Theocast, 28 Jan 2026](https://theocast.org/blogs/articles/why-christians-struggle-to-read-the-bible-1)
- Stopping because of harm: a Christian writer stopped reading the Bible because leaders had used it to justify positions she found harmful (LGBTQ+ issues, politics). She returned through studying in a community and through Zach Lambert's *Better Ways to Read the Bible*. Quote: "I no longer believe God is mad at me, or waiting to smite me." — Shannan Martin (date not captured) — [Substack](https://shannanmartin.substack.com/p/why-i-stopped-reading-the-bible)
- Commentary on exvangelicals argues that leavers often know the Bible well but disagree with how evangelicals interpret it — [Religion News Service, Aug 2021](https://religionnews.com/2021/08/28/evangelicals-youre-still-not-really-listening-to-what-exvangelicals-are-saying/)

### Inferences
- Streaks and plan calendars are a double-edged design pattern: they build the habit and then cause the drop-out. A missed day should not read as failure.
- "Reading without retaining" comes up together with pace. Slower, smaller units plus a moment of reflection is the recurring fix.
- Comprehension and confidence are split: people feel they could defend the Bible but cannot explain a given passage. That is a gap at the level of the individual passage.

### Gaps
- No direct lay evidence (Reddit or r/exchristian) on contradictory interpretations, not knowing whom to trust, or resource overwhelm. These are common themes, but no citable lay posts could be reached.
- No quantified data on the Leviticus and Numbers drop-off (for example, plan completion by day). App analytics would be needed.

## 3. What do beginners ask most often?

### Takeaway
"Where do I start?" is the most common beginner question, and the data backs it up: 17% of Americans who want to read more name it as their barrier. "Which translation?" is the next most common and comes with KJV-versus-modern confusion. The usual advice converges: start with a Gospel (Mark or John), then short books (James, 1 John), and avoid starting at Genesis.

### Cited Findings
- 17% cite "I don't know where to start" as a barrier — [Decision Magazine on ABS 2025](https://decisionmagazine.com/more-than-half-of-americans-wish-they-would-read-the-bible-more)
- Not knowing where to start, or jumping around until nothing sticks, is named as one of the most common struggles. The advice is to pick one short book (James, Titus, 1 John) and stay with it — [Rod Trent Substack](https://rodtrent.substack.com/p/a-beginners-guide-to-studying-the); [Bible Study Tip #2](https://oorlaoshilaja.substack.com/p/bible-study-tip-2-choose-a-book-and)
- Common starting advice: one of the Gospels, often Mark because it is fast-paced; or Psalms and Romans for comfort; not Genesis, because Leviticus derails beginners — [Noel in UAE Substack](https://noelinuae.substack.com/p/i-want-to-start-reading-the-bible-where-do-i-begin-317772)
- Translation: a 2025 piece argues beginners need translations made with them in mind. Comparison guides present KJV's archaic syntax as a barrier and NIV or NLT as easier on-ramps — [Word in Black, July 2025](https://wordinblack.com/2025/07/beginners-need-bible-translations-with-them-in-mind/); [Bible in a Year comparison](https://bibleinyear.com/bible-translation-comparison)
- 15% name "language difficult to relate to" as a barrier — [Decision Magazine](https://decisionmagazine.com/more-than-half-of-americans-wish-they-would-read-the-bible-more)
- A lay forum thread titled "Do you struggle with which version to use?" exists on PuritanBoard. It was not fetchable (403) — [PuritanBoard](https://puritanboard.com/goto/post?id=609477)

### Inferences
- The two first-run questions an app must answer without friction are "where do I start" and "which translation". Both are about getting started, not about depth.

### Gaps
- Could not rank beginner question frequency from community data (for example, counting r/Bible titles), because Reddit was blocked. "How do I understand context" and "what it means versus what people say it means" are plausible frequent questions, but unverified here.

## 4. What helps understanding?

### Takeaway
The recurring things that unlock understanding are: the big-picture storyline (BibleProject overviews), context and continuity, slowing down, community or study groups, and a mentor. Healthier framing helps too: relationship rather than scoreboard. For AI help, practising Christians want sources visible and checkable.

### Cited Findings
- State of the Bible 2025 reports that Bible study groups "prove helpful" for flourishing outcomes — [Scripture Engagement](https://scripture-engagement.org/content/state-of-the-bible-usa-2025/)
- BibleProject is described as helping people experience the Bible "as a unified story that leads to Jesus". A church uses it as its on-ramp for exactly that reason — [Journey Chattanooga](https://journeychattanooga.com/bible/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BibleProject)
- Advice in answer to "I need help understanding the Bible" includes finding a mentor (a pastor or Christian friend) and starting with John — [Patch community post](https://patch.com/illinois/libertyville/i-need-help-understanding-the-bible)
- A ministry diagnosis says the missing piece is "tools for understanding context and continuity", alongside community — [Theocast, Jan 2026](https://theocast.org/blogs/articles/why-christians-struggle-to-read-the-bible-1)
- Reframing reading from performance to relationship is what restored the habit for Coombs ([YMI 2023](https://ymi.today/2023/06/its-time-to-take-the-guilt-out-of-your-bible-reading/)). A lay poster on ChristianityBoard shares Shane Pruitt's line that Bible reading "isn't about completing a goal" ([ChristianityBoard, Jan 2025](https://www.christianityboard.com/threads/bible-reading-plans.67257/)). The same thread is quoted in section 1; one quote per source is kept.
- On AI, commentators recommend chatbots that link to their sources, and checking any surprising "fact" against a standard commentary — [Christianity.com](https://www.christianity.com/wiki/current-events/can-christians-trust-ai-for-bible-study.html). An earlier Barna finding, reported secondhand: 83% of practising Christians are concerned about AI misinterpreting Scripture — [same source](https://www.christianity.com/wiki/current-events/can-christians-trust-ai-for-bible-study.html)
- For one returner, community study and a reading approach centred on healing were what brought her back — [Shannan Martin](https://shannanmartin.substack.com/p/why-i-stopped-reading-the-bible)

### Inferences
- The things that unlock understanding fall into three types:
  - Orientation: where this passage sits in the story, and who wrote it to whom.
  - Pace: smaller units and time to reflect.
  - Social proof: a group or mentor, or seeing what trusted others think.
- An explanation layer that shows its sources and where interpretations differ would answer the trust gap people state about AI.

### Gaps
- No lay "it finally clicked" testimonials could be captured verbatim. These are very common on Reddit and YouTube comments but were inaccessible. Treat "BibleProject and big-picture context made it click" as strongly suggested by secondary sources, not proven by lay quotes here.
- No evidence was found on the specific value of cross-references, author and audience notes, or side-by-side interpretations as lay readers experience them.

## 5. How do lay readers feel about Greek and Hebrew tools?

### Takeaway
Original-language tools attract curious laypeople, mainly through Strong's numbers and interlinears. Teachers and scholars, however, consistently warn that lay word studies produce the root fallacy, "meaning soup" (piling every listed sense into one word) and the word-concept fallacy. The community framing is "a little Greek is a dangerous thing". Some do say it is an eye-opener for those who respect what they don't know.

### Cited Findings
- Mark Ward (Logos) gives three reasons to move beyond Strong's:
  - Its glosses are treated as interchangeable definitions.
  - It invites "meaning soup".
  - It leads readers into word-study fallacies; context, not etymology, decides meaning.
  Quote: "Words mean what they are used to mean in any given time period" — [Logos, 4 Oct 2022](https://www.logos.com/grow/better-resources-than-strongs-concordance/)
- Looking up a Greek word in Strong's and declaring its "real meaning" is what D. A. Carson calls the root fallacy. Assuming one word equals the whole biblical concept is the word-concept ("concordance") fallacy — [ChurchLeaders, Nicholas McDonald, "7 ways bad word study"](https://churchleaders.com/pastors/pastor-articles/166406-nicholas-mcdonald-7-ways-bad-word-study.html); [The Aquila Report](https://theaquilareport.com/exegetical-fallacies-the-word-study-fallacy/)
- On the B-Greek mailing list, discussion of "Greek for laymen" uses the "little Greek is a dangerous thing" framing. It also says that, for some, Greek opens a lifetime of exploration, and that the benefit depends on the learner respecting the limits of what they know. These posts are from 2004, outside the preferred window, and the list is mixed lay and academic — [B-Greek archive, Mar 2004](https://www.ibiblio.org/bgreek/lists.ibiblio.org/2004-March/028706.html)
- A blog warns lay readers to "take care how you use your resources" in word studies — [Knowable Word, Jan 2022](https://knowableword.com/2022/01/05/take-care-how-you-use-your-resources)

### Inferences
- A lay-facing original-language feature should show what a word means in this verse, with its grammar from source data. It should not show an etymology or a list of every gloss. Otherwise it walks users into the fallacies that teachers warn about.
- The warnings come almost entirely from teachers. The lay side of the feeling (intimidation versus delight) is under-evidenced here.

### Gaps
- No 2022–2026 lay testimony on whether Greek and Hebrew tools intimidated or helped them, because Reddit and ChristianForums were blocked. This is a priority gap to fill with a manual Reddit pass: r/Bible and r/Christianity, search "Strong's", "interlinear", "learn Greek".
