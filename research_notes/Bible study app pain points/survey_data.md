# Survey data on Bible engagement, comprehension and barriers (2022–2026)

Research date: 2026-10-10. US first, UK second. Figures are quoted as published. Where I could only find a figure in secondary press coverage, the note says so.

Methodology reference for the American Bible Society (ABS) "State of the Bible" (SOTB) series:
- SOTB 2024: NORC AmeriSpeak panel, online and phone, 2,506 US adults, fielded January 4–23, 2024, MoE ±2.73% — [SOTB 2024 PDF, Appendix 1](https://1s712.americanbible.org/state-of-the-bible/stateofthebible/State_of_the_bible-2024.pdf)
- SOTB 2025: 2,656 online interviews, January 2–21, 2025, NORC — [ABS press release](https://www.americanbible.org/news/press-releases/articles/sotb-2025-release/)
- SOTB 2026: 2,649 online and phone interviews, January 8–27, 2026, NORC AmeriSpeak, all 50 states and DC — [ABS press release, SOTB 2026 ch. 1](https://www.americanbible.org/news/press-releases/articles/state-of-the-bible-2026-chapter-1/)

Definitions (SOTB 2024 Appendix 2): a "Bible User" uses the Bible at least three or four times a year outside church services. The Scripture Engagement Scale is "Based on responses to 14 survey items about the frequency of Bible use and the impact and centrality its message", centred on 100 with SD 15; "Scripture Engaged" means a score of 100 or more. The Movable Middle sits between Scripture Engaged and Bible Disengaged. — [SOTB 2024 PDF](https://1s712.americanbible.org/state-of-the-bible/stateofthebible/State_of_the_bible-2024.pdf). Press coverage describes the measure as "a 15-question metric" — [Baptist Press, 2024](https://www.baptistpress.com/resource-library/news/state-of-the-bible-americans-less-engaged-but-gen-z-bucking-trend/). The report's own appendix says 14 items, so I'd use 14.

## 1. American Bible Society State of the Bible 2023–2026: Bible users, engagement, Movable Middle, Gen Z, barriers

### Takeaway
US Bible use fell from 50% (2021) to 40% (2022) and then to a record low of 38% in 2024. It rose to 41% in 2025 and fell back in 2026: 25% active and 13% occasional users, which adds up to 38%. Scripture engagement followed the same path: 18% in 2024, 20% in 2025, 17% in 2026. The Movable Middle, people who are curious but uncertain, grew to 28% in 2026. Lack of time and not knowing where to start are the barriers ABS names most often. "Don't know where to start" is the top barrier for the Movable Middle and for younger adults.

### Cited Findings

**Bible users trend**
- Adult Bible Users by year (millions / %): 2011 118 / 50%; 2014 129 / 53%; 2019 124 / 49%; 2020 123 / 48%; 2021 128 / 50%; 2022 103 / 40%; 2023 100 / 39%; 2024 99 / 38% — [SOTB 2024 PDF, ch. 1 chart "Adult Bible Users 2011–2024"](https://1s712.americanbible.org/state-of-the-bible/stateofthebible/State_of_the_bible-2024.pdf)
- 2022 survey: "Bible Use tumbling by ten points (down to 40%) with non-users increasing by that same amount (up to 40%)" — [SOTB 2024 PDF](https://1s712.americanbible.org/state-of-the-bible/stateofthebible/State_of_the_bible-2024.pdf)
- 2024 self-report: 15% said their Bible use increased in the past year and 10% said it decreased. Gen Z: 21% increased, 9% decreased. Millennials were the only group where more decreased (12%) than increased (11%). ABS warns this "probably indicates that people want to think they've been reading the Bible more" — [SOTB 2024 PDF](https://1s712.americanbible.org/state-of-the-bible/stateofthebible/State_of_the_bible-2024.pdf)
- 2025: "The number of Bible Users has increased this year from 38% to 41%, which translates to 10 million more American adults" — [ABS SOTB 2025 release](https://www.americanbible.org/news/press-releases/articles/sotb-2025-release/). Press coverage gives 110 million in 2025 against 99 million in 2024, and 49% / 124 million in 2019 — [Baptist Press summary via search](https://www.baptistpress.com/resource-library/news/state-of-the-bible-10-million-more-adults-using-scripture-but-numbers-lag-behind-pre-covid-levels/print/)
- 2025: Millennials saw "a 29% increase in Bible Use from 2024 to 2025 and men saw a 19% increase". These are relative increases, not percentage points — [ABS SOTB 2025 release](https://www.americanbible.org/news/press-releases/articles/sotb-2025-release/)
- 2026: 25% active Bible users, 13% occasional, 62% don't read Scripture. Men who are active users: 24%, up from 22% in 2024. Some women moved from active to occasional use — [Baptist Press, Apr 2026](https://www.baptistpress.com/resource-library/news/state-of-the-bible-growth-in-bible-use-reverses-in-2026-but-trend-uncertain/) (secondary; ABS's own page was not fetched in full)

**Scripture engagement and the Movable Middle**
- 2024: Scripture Engaged "held steady in the past year at about 47 million (18%)". The Movable Middle "lost four percentage points in the last year (29% to 25%). This represents more than 10 million people who dropped into... the Bible Disengaged". Bible Disengaged was "at its highest point ever" — [SOTB 2024 PDF](https://1s712.americanbible.org/state-of-the-bible/stateofthebible/State_of_the_bible-2024.pdf). Bible Disengaged was 57%, or 151 million — [Baptist Press, 2024](https://www.baptistpress.com/resource-library/news/state-of-the-bible-americans-less-engaged-but-gen-z-bucking-trend/)
- 2024 by generation (Scripture Engaged / Movable Middle / Bible Disengaged): Gen Z adults 11% / 27% / 61%; Millennials 12% / 23% / 65%; Gen X 21% / 21% / 58%; Boomers+ 24% / 27% / 49% (chart values; the text confirms Gen Z 11%, Gen Z Movable Middle 27%, Millennials 65% disengaged, Boomers 24%) — [SOTB 2024 PDF](https://1s712.americanbible.org/state-of-the-bible/stateofthebible/State_of_the_bible-2024.pdf)
- 2025: Scripture Engaged among Gen Z went from 11% in 2024 to 15% in 2025. Millennials went from 12% to 17% — [ABS SOTB 2025 release](https://www.americanbible.org/news/press-releases/articles/sotb-2025-release/)
- 2025: the Movable Middle numbers 71 million people. 54% of US adults were Scripture-disengaged in 2025, against 47% in 2019 — [Baptist Press summary via search, 2025](https://www.baptistpress.com/resource-library/news/state-of-the-bible-10-million-more-adults-using-scripture-but-numbers-lag-behind-pre-covid-levels/print/)
- 2025: "80% of Americans in the Movable Middle... wish they read the Bible more". "56% of all Americans and 82% of the Movable Middle" are curious about the Bible or Jesus — [ABS SOTB 2025 release](https://www.americanbible.org/news/press-releases/articles/sotb-2025-release/)
- 2026: Scripture Engaged fell back to "levels close to those of 2024 (17%)". Press reports give 20% for 2025 — [ABS SOTB 2026 ch. 1](https://www.americanbible.org/news/press-releases/articles/state-of-the-bible-2026-chapter-1/); [Baptist Press, 2026](https://www.baptistpress.com/resource-library/news/state-of-the-bible-growth-in-bible-use-reverses-in-2026-but-trend-uncertain/)
- 2026: Movable Middle is "28% of the population, an increase of 9 million people since 2024". The Disengaged segment shrank by "5 million adults over the last two years" — [ABS SOTB 2026 ch. 1](https://www.americanbible.org/news/press-releases/articles/state-of-the-bible-2026-chapter-1/)
- 2026 by race: Scripture Engaged 29% of Black adults, 16% of white adults, 15% of Hispanic adults — [Baptist Press, 2026](https://www.baptistpress.com/resource-library/news/state-of-the-bible-growth-in-bible-use-reverses-in-2026-but-trend-uncertain/)
- 2024: "Do you wish you used the Bible more?" Yes: all respondents 53%, Bible Users 84%, non-Bible Users 34% — [SOTB 2024 PDF](https://1s712.americanbible.org/state-of-the-bible/stateofthebible/State_of_the_bible-2024.pdf)
- 2024 Gen Z: 50% of Gen Z adults agreed the Bible's message had transformed their lives in the prior year's survey, rising to 54% in 2024 — [Baptist Press, 2024](https://www.baptistpress.com/resource-library/news/state-of-the-bible-americans-less-engaged-but-gen-z-bucking-trend/)

**How much of the Bible people have read (2026)**
- "Half of Americans say they have read half of the Bible or more". "One-third... have read all or most". 17% say they have read it in its entirety. "Only 10% of Americans say they have not read any of the Bible" — [ABS SOTB 2026 ch. 1](https://www.americanbible.org/news/press-releases/articles/state-of-the-bible-2026-chapter-1/)
- 64% of those who say the Bible has transformed their life have read most or all of it. 60% of those who agree "The Bible was written to control or manipulate" have read little or none of it — [ABS SOTB 2026 ch. 1](https://www.americanbible.org/news/press-releases/articles/state-of-the-bible-2026-chapter-1/)

**Barriers and frustrations**
- SOTB 2023, Bible Users' top frustrations with reading on their own: "Not enough time" 26%, "Don't know where to start" 17%, "Lack of excitement about reading the Bible" 16%, "Difficult relating to language" 15%, "Layout difficult to navigate" 9%, "Stories are confusing" 8% — [Kathy Howard blog citing SOTB 2023](https://www.kathyhoward.org/americans-top-6-frustrations-with-bible-reading/) (secondary; not checked against the ABS PDF)
- SOTB 2025 (church edition): for the Movable Middle, "having enough time to read the Bible and knowing where to start are the biggest barriers". 43% of church attenders "don't consistently interact with the Bible in a way that shapes their choices" — [ABS State of the Bible in the Church 2025](https://www.americanbible.org/sotb-church-2025/)
- The Movable Middle cites not knowing where to start as its biggest frustration, followed by not enough time, language difficult to relate to, and layout difficult to navigate. "I don't know where to start" was a top answer for Gen Z and Millennials — [search summary of ABS / NRB coverage](https://nrb.org/state-of-the-bible-the-bible-in-america/) (no percentages found)
- 2024 comprehension proxy: "only about a third of the public say they're 'moderately' or 'highly' knowledgeable about the Bible" — [SOTB 2024 PDF, AI section](https://1s712.americanbible.org/state-of-the-bible/stateofthebible/State_of_the_bible-2024.pdf)

### Inferences
- The 2025 rise in Bible use did not hold. In 2026, Bible use (25% + 13% = 38%) and engagement (17%) were back at 2024 levels. The Movable Middle (28%) kept growing, so curiosity rose while reading fell. An app aimed at curious people who don't know where to start fits the largest group that is open to the Bible.
- "Don't know where to start", "language hard to relate to" and "layout hard to navigate" together are a comprehension and navigation problem. In the 2023 data they add up to more than lack of time. They overlap, though, so the shares can't be summed.

### Gaps
- I could not reach the primary ABS 2023, 2025 or 2026 PDFs, so I have no barrier percentages for 2024–2026. Only the 2023 percentages exist, and only through a secondary source. The full SOTB 2025 and 2026 reports should have segment-level barrier tables.
- No published figure for an "average scripture engagement score". ABS publishes segment shares.
- The 2025 "71 million" Movable Middle figure and the 54% disengaged figure come from press coverage, not the ABS page.

## 2. Barna Group: Bible reading, digital use, Bible and AI; what people use to understand the Bible

### Takeaway
Barna's tracking found weekly Bible reading at a 25-year low of 30% in 2024 and a rebound to 42% in 2025. Gen Z (49%) and Millennials (50%) drove the rise, and young men now outread young women. On AI: 74% of US adults worry about AI misinterpreting Scripture, yet about 40% of practicing Christians say AI has helped their Bible study, prayer or spiritual growth.

### Cited Findings
- Weekly Bible reading among US adults: 39% (2000), 30% (2024, 25-year low), 42% (2025) — [Barna, "How Millennials and Gen Z Are Driving a Bible Reading Comeback"](https://www.barna.com/trends/bible-reading-trends/)
- 2025 by generation: Gen Z 49% (up from 30%), Millennials 50% (up 16 points), Gen X 41%, Boomers 31% (down from a 49% peak in 2010). Self-identified Christians: 50% — [Barna](https://www.barna.com/trends/bible-reading-trends/). One secondary search summary printed Boomers as "3%", which looks like a typo for 31%.
- 2025 by gender: Gen Z men 54% vs Gen Z women 46%; Millennial men 57% vs Millennial women 43% — [Religion News Service, Nov 2025](https://religionnews.com/2025/11/06/new-study-shows-how-millennials-and-gen-z-are-driving-a-bible-reading-comeback/) (via search summary)
- Belief that the Bible is "totally accurate in the principles it teaches" (strongly agree): 43% in 2000, 36% in 2025 (general population), 44% of self-identified Christians — [Barna](https://www.barna.com/trends/bible-reading-trends/)
- Barna 2025 methodology: 12,116 online interviews, January–October 2025, quota sampled. The full 25-year tracking totals 138,556 interviews. Part of the State of the Church 2025 study with Gloo — [Barna](https://www.barna.com/trends/bible-reading-trends/)
- Barna / Gloo AI study: US adults n=1,514 (online, November 2025) and US Protestant pastors n=442 (online, December 2025). Concern about AI interpreting Scripture incorrectly: 74% of US adults, 83% of practicing Christians, 94% of pastors. Only 5% of pastors think AI should avoid contested Scripture — [Business Wire, Aug 2026](https://secure.businesswire.com/news/home/20260826106650/en/Only-5-of-Pastors-Think-AI-Should-Avoid-Contested-Scripture-Despite-Widespread-Concern-About-Misinterpretation) (via search summary)
- Barna / Gloo, Feb 2026: 48% of practicing US Christians say they would trust AI with their spiritual growth. 34% say AI's spiritual guidance is as trustworthy as a pastor's (Gen Z 39%, Millennials 44%) — [Business Wire, Feb 2026](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260219270610/en/ai-is-becoming-a-spiritual-authority-in-americans%e2%80%99-lives-new-research-reveals/) (via search summary; page returned 403)
- Same research as reported by Crosswalk: "About 40 percent of practicing Christians say AI has helped them with Bible study, prayer, or spiritual growth". 31% of US adults trust AI spiritual advice as much as pastoral guidance. 41% of pastors use AI for Bible study. Only 12% of pastors feel comfortable teaching about AI — [Crosswalk](https://www.crosswalk.com/headlines/contributors/michael-foust/4-in-10-christians-say-ai-has-helped-them-with-bible-study-prayer.html). The 34% (practicing Christians) and 31% (US adults) figures describe different populations.
- SOTB 2024 (ABS/NORC) attitudes to AI, general public: "AI can enhance my spiritual practices and promote spiritual health": 9% agree, 68% disagree. "AI can aid in moral reasoning": 13% agree, 58% disagree. "The use of AI goes against biblical teaching": 20% agree, 40% uncertain, 40% disagree — [SOTB 2024 PDF](https://1s712.americanbible.org/state-of-the-bible/stateofthebible/State_of_the_bible-2024.pdf)

### Inferences
- Attitudes to AI shifted fast between the SOTB January 2024 survey (9% think AI can enhance spiritual practice) and Barna's November 2025 survey (around 40% of practicing Christians say AI has helped). The two surveys use different populations and wording, so this is a direction, not a measured change. Worry about AI getting Scripture wrong stays very high (74–94%). Grounding answers in sources and showing provenance is how to answer that worry.

### Gaps
- I found no recent Barna figure ranking the tools people use to understand the Bible (study Bibles, commentaries, pastors, YouTube, AI). That needs a targeted search of barna.com or the ABS "Bible resources" questions.
- No Barna print-vs-digital split for 2025 found.

## 3. Lifeway Research: reading habits, whole-Bible completion, discipleship predictors

### Takeaway
31% of Protestant churchgoers read the Bible daily. 22% of Americans have read the whole Bible at least once, rising to 59% among people who attend church more than weekly. In Lifeway's Transformational Discipleship research, Bible reading has been among the strongest predictors of spiritual growth.

### Cited Findings
- State of Discipleship survey of 2,130 US Protestant churchgoers, March 19–26, 2025, sampling error ±2.21%: 31% read the Bible daily, 30% a few times a week (61% regular), 14% once a week, 11% a few times a month, 5% once a month, 9% rarely or never — [Lifeway Research, Feb 10 2026](https://research.lifeway.com/2026/02/10/fewer-than-1-in-3-churchgoers-read-the-bible-daily/)
- "9 in 10 Americans have read at least some of the Bible, but just 22% have finished the book at least once". Among those attending more than once a week, 59% have read the entire Bible at least once — [Lifeway Research, 2026](https://research.lifeway.com/2026/02/10/fewer-than-1-in-3-churchgoers-read-the-bible-daily/). The 22% comes from an earlier Lifeway study cited in this article; its original year and sample were not given on the page. Compare ABS 2026: 17% have read the whole Bible.
- Churchgoers after several days without Bible reading: 62% "desperately miss the time with God" (30% strongly agree); 58% feel "unfulfilled" (26% strongly agree). 74% say the Bible has authority over every area of their lives (40% strongly). 72% think about biblical truths through the day (31% strongly) — [Lifeway Research, 2026](https://research.lifeway.com/2026/02/10/fewer-than-1-in-3-churchgoers-read-the-bible-daily/)
- Bible engagement is one of eight signposts of maturing believers: engaging the Bible, obeying God and denying self, serving God and others, sharing Christ, exercising faith, seeking God, building relationships, living unashamed — [Lifeway Research, 2026](https://research.lifeway.com/2026/02/10/fewer-than-1-in-3-churchgoers-read-the-bible-daily/)
- Transformational Discipleship: reading the Bible was the most predictive element of increased spiritual maturity in Lifeway's first national study. In the 2011 assessment of 2,930 Protestant churchgoers, Bible reading was again most predictive — [Lifeway Research, 2013](https://research.lifeway.com/2013/03/26/leading-the-church-to-spiritual-transformation/); [Lifeway News, 2011](https://news.lifeway.com/2011/10/01/bible-reading-leads-to-spiritual-growth/) (pre-2022; the date range is relaxed because it's foundational)

### Inferences
- Whole-Bible completion is low even among committed churchgoers. About 41% of people who attend more than weekly have never finished it, since 59% have. Plans that cover the whole canon have a large unserved base.

### Gaps
- I did not find a 2022–2026 Lifeway figure on what churchgoers struggle with in understanding Scripture (for example "difficult to understand").
- The original source year for "22% have finished the Bible" was not on the page.

## 4. UK and Europe

### Takeaway
The UK's best-known recent data point, Bible Society's "The Quiet Revival" (April 2025, YouGov), was withdrawn in March 2026 after YouGov found fraudulent respondents in the 2024 sample. Treat its church attendance and Bible-reading growth figures as unreliable. Physical Bible sales in the UK did rise sharply between 2019 and 2025.

### Cited Findings
- Original Quiet Revival claims (April 2025): church attendance in England and Wales rose 56% between 2018 and 2024. Attendance among 18–24 year-olds rose from 4% to 16% — [Premier Christianity](https://www.premierchristianity.com/news-analysis/was-the-quiet-revival-fake-news/21379.article)
- From the same report: 79% of adults in England and Wales hardly ever or never read the Bible — [Scripture Engagement summary of The Quiet Revival](https://scripture-engagement.org/content/the-quiet-revival/) (comes from the withdrawn dataset, so it is unreliable)
- Withdrawal: in March 2026, YouGov "re-analysed the data using new tools and techniques" and found "a number of respondents who we can now identify as fraudulent". YouGov accepted "full responsibility" and said Bible Society had reported its data accurately. Bible Society replaced the report with "The Quiet Revival One Year On: What's the Story?" — [Premier Christianity](https://www.premierchristianity.com/news-analysis/was-the-quiet-revival-fake-news/21379.article); [Religion News Service, Apr 1 2026](https://religionnews.com/2026/04/01/uks-bible-society-on-the-defensive-after-retracting-report-on-revival/)
- UK physical Bible sales rose 106% between 2019 and 2025, with 27.7% growth from 2024 to 2025. Sales went from £2.69 million (2019) to £6.3 million (2025). The ESV was the top-selling translation — [Christian Post / Christian Today on 2025 Bible sales](https://www.christiantoday.com/news/bible-sales-grew-in-2025-data-shows) (via search summary; underlying source probably Circana/Nielsen BookScan, not verified)

### Inferences
- There is no reliable 2024–2026 measure of UK Bible-reading frequency right now. Sales data is the only hard signal of rising interest, and buying a Bible is not the same as reading it.

### Gaps
- No reliable post-withdrawal UK reading-frequency or comprehension figures found. YouGov was reported to be repeating the study; results were not found.
- No continental Europe Bible engagement survey data (2022–2026) found within the search budget.

## 5. App usage: YouVersion stats, reading plan completion and drop-off

### Takeaway
YouVersion reports more than 1 billion installs, a record 19 million people opening the app on one day (November 2, 2025), and 3 million one-year plan sign-ups on New Year's Day 2025. It does not publish plan completion rates. Its most-completed plans run a week or less. In SOTB 2026, Bible users who follow a plan were far more likely to have read most or all of the Bible (74%).

### Cited Findings
- YouVersion 2025 year in review (press release, December 4, 2025): "more than one billion installs". 19 million people opened the app on November 2, the all-time single-day record. More than 3 million people subscribed to one-year Bible Plans on New Year's Day, up 18% on 2024. "In an average second, there are 40 Bible Plan days completed and 112 Bible verses highlighted, bookmarked, and noted". 2.6 million took the 30-Day Bible Challenge. Daily Bible use in November was up 19% on November 2024. Daily-use growth: Sub-Saharan Africa +27%; North Africa, Middle East and Central Asia +33%; North America +14% — [PR Newswire, YouVersion 2025 Verse of the Year](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/youversion-announces-2025-verse-of-the-year-as-bible-engagement-reaches-new-heights-globally-302632694.html)
- Verse of the Year 2025: Isaiah 41:10, the most-engaged verse for the fourth time in six years. Also near the top: Jeremiah 29:11 and Romans 12:2. Most searched terms: love, anxiety, peace — [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/youversion-announces-2025-verse-of-the-year-as-bible-engagement-reaches-new-heights-globally-302632694.html); [ChurchLeaders](https://churchleaders.com/news/2209898-youversion-2025-verse-of-the-year-bobby-gruenewald.html)
- Easter Sunday 2025 overtook January 5 as YouVersion's biggest Bible-engagement day, with nearly 19 million people — [Crosswalk](https://www.crosswalk.com/headlines/contributors/michael-foust/youversion-reveals-2025s-most-popular-verse-as-bible-reading-hits-record-levels.html) (via search summary)
- "The top 10 most completed Bible plans... take only a week or less to complete" — [Christian Post](https://assets.christianpost.com/news/youversion-reveals-top-bible-verse-of-2025.html) (via search summary)
- Format, SOTB 2025: "One-third of Bible Users access the Bible only in print". "Two-thirds... access the Bible digitally at least some of the time". "Three out of five digital Bible Users (62%) use Bible apps" — [ABS SOTB 2025 release](https://www.americanbible.org/news/press-releases/articles/sotb-2025-release/)
- Format, SOTB 2026: nearly 80% of Bible users read a printed Bible at least monthly, and 62% use digital text monthly. "Almost 75% of Bible Users" among Gen Z and Millennials read in both print and digital at least monthly. For younger generations, digital slightly exceeds print — [ABS SOTB 2026 ch. 1](https://www.americanbible.org/news/press-releases/articles/state-of-the-bible-2026-chapter-1/); [Christian Daily](https://www.christiandaily.com/news/bible-reading-declines-as-openness-to-scripture-rises-in-us-new-state-of-the-bible-report-finds)
- Plans and completion, SOTB 2026: "Nearly three-quarters (74%)" of people who use reading plans "have read most or all of the Bible" — [ABS SOTB 2026 ch. 1](https://www.americanbible.org/news/press-releases/articles/state-of-the-bible-2026-chapter-1/)
- **Unverified, do not cite as fact:** claims that "only about 8–9%" of people who start a one-year plan finish it, that traffic drops "30 percent in the first week", and that group reading lifts completion to "65% or higher". They appear on SEO/aggregator pages that attribute them loosely to "LifeWay Research, Bible Gateway, and YouVersion". I found no primary source. Search summary also reported "over 670 million total plan completions" for YouVersion, which is unverified. An older Christianity Today piece (2014) discusses Bible-in-a-year drop-off — [Christianity Today, 2014](https://www.christianitytoday.com/2014/02/read-through-bible-in-year/) (not fetched; outside the date range)

### Inferences
- Short plans dominate completions and one-year plans dominate January sign-ups. The stretch between signing up and finishing is where products win or lose. That pattern is consistent with the evidence here, but no measured completion rate exists.
- Print is still the most common format even among app users. Most younger users use both, so an app competes for part of a reader's time, not for exclusive use.

### Gaps
- No primary published plan completion or drop-off rate from YouVersion, Bible Gateway or Lifeway.
- No YouVersion "most-shared verse" figure separate from "most engaged".
- No primary audio-Bible usage share for 2025–2026 found.

## 6. What helps comprehension and habit formation

### Takeaway
Correlational evidence links lasting engagement to disciplined practices: following a plan, reading at the same time each day, reading whole chapters or stories, and using a study guide or commentary. Casual practices (a few verses at a time, picking passages by mood) are common across every segment and do not separate the engaged from the rest. Frequency matters, and the older "Power of 4" research found effects rising sharply at four or more days a week.

### Cited Findings
- SOTB 2024 split six reading habits into "Casual" (reading a few verses at a time, choosing passages by mood) and "Disciplined" (reading one or more chapters or a whole story, following a schedule, plan or program, reading at about the same time each day, using a study guide or commentary). The Scripture Engaged "far exceed the Movable Middle and the Bible Disengaged in the four practices we call Disciplined". The Movable Middle slightly outpaces the Engaged on "few verses at a time" (52% to 51%). Thriving Christians on the Spiritual Vitality Gauge used disciplined methods far more. ABS suggests "perhaps it would help to get a Bible study app that will prompt you each day, or to start reading full chapters rather than select verses" — [SOTB 2024 PDF, ch. 2](https://1s712.americanbible.org/state-of-the-bible/stateofthebible/State_of_the_bible-2024.pdf)
- SOTB 2025 church edition: Scripture Engaged people typically "set aside time each day, follow a plan, and lean into Bible reading resources for support" — [ABS State of the Bible in the Church 2025](https://www.americanbible.org/sotb-church-2025/)
- SOTB 2026: 74% of plan users have read most or all of the Bible — [ABS SOTB 2026 ch. 1](https://www.americanbible.org/news/press-releases/articles/state-of-the-bible-2026-chapter-1/)
- "Power of 4" (Center for Bible Engagement, Cole and Ovwigho; survey of about 40,000 people aged 8 to 80+, 2009). People engaging Scripture at least four times a week reported: feeling lonely −30%, anger issues −32%, bitterness in relationships −40%, alcoholism −57%, sex outside marriage −68%, feeling spiritually stagnant −60%, viewing pornography −61%, sharing faith +200%, discipling others +230% — [Gospel Herald / Aquila Report summary](https://www.gospelherald.com/news/new-year-brings-new-hope-for-bible-literacy); [Back to the Bible research](https://backtothebible.org/research) (2009, outside the date range; correlational and self-reported)
- Lifeway: Bible reading most predictive of spiritual growth (2011, n=2,930) — [Lifeway News, 2011](https://news.lifeway.com/2011/10/01/bible-reading-leads-to-spiritual-growth/)

### Inferences
- The evidence favours product features that build disciplined habits: a daily prompt at a set time, reading by chapter or story rather than by verse, a plan, and a study aid or context alongside the text. All the evidence is correlational. People who are already engaged may simply pick these methods.

### Gaps
- No 2022–2026 quantitative study found on reading with others, audio's effect on comprehension, or how historical and cultural context affects understanding. The plan-completion uplift from group reading circulates online without a primary source.
- No published randomised or longitudinal study of Bible apps' effect on comprehension found.
